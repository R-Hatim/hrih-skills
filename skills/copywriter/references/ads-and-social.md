## 1. Hooks

The first line (or the first second of a video) decides whether anything else gets read. Strong hooks are specific:
- **Call out the audience:** "If you run payroll for under 20 people…"
- **A concrete result:** "We cut our onboarding from 9 emails to 3 and activation went up." Only if real.
- **A true observation that cuts against the usual advice**, stated as a fact, not as "Everyone's wrong about X".
- **A real customer's words.**
- **"How we…"** with a number.

Avoid these hook templates: "Here's the thing", "Hot take:", "Unpopular opinion:", "Let that sink in", "Stop doing X", "Most people don't know this", and "I [did X]. Here's what happened:" with a list below. They have become as recognizable as the AI patterns in ai-tells.md.

## 2. Search ads (Google responsive search ads)

- **Limits:** up to 15 headlines of ≤ 30 characters; up to 4 descriptions of ≤ 90 characters; display paths ≤ 15 characters each. Count characters exactly and report the count next to each line.
- **Combinable:** assets are shown in any order, so each headline must make sense alone and next to any other. Avoid two headlines that say the same thing.
- **Varied angles:** keyword match, benefit, problem, offer, proof (real only), audience, shipping or returns, CTA. Put any required disclaimer in a description.
- **Message match:** the landing page H1 should continue the strongest headline.

## 3. Paid social (Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok, and similar)

- Primary text shows roughly the first 125 characters before truncation. Put the hook and the offer there. Headline fields often show about 25–40 characters. Placements vary, so check current specs.
- Give several distinct **angles**, not rewordings of one line: pain, outcome, proof, objection, identity. Label each variant with its angle so the user can test them.
- For video, the on-screen text, the first spoken line and the first frame should all say the same one thing.
- **Policy:**
  - Don't address personal attributes ("Are you overweight?", "Struggling with debt?"). Platforms reject these.
  - Don't make unsubstantiated health or finance claims.
  - Use real deadlines only.

## 4. Organic posts

- **The first line is the post.** Feeds cut after one to three lines, so the opening must stand alone and make the next line worth expanding.
- **One idea per post.** A specific moment, number or decision beats generic advice. A story with a real detail beats a list of tips.
- **LinkedIn:**
  - first person, in the person's own voice (use their samples)
  - short paragraphs, but not one sentence per line throughout ("broetry" is a recognizable template)
  - a concrete lesson or result
  - no engagement bait ("Agree?", "Comment YES")
  - at most a few relevant hashtags at the end, or none
- **X:** one sharp, specific claim. In threads, each post stands on its own.
- **Instagram:** the caption supports the visual. Hook in the first line; keep hashtags few and relevant.
- **Brand accounts** follow the brand voice. **Personal and founder accounts** follow that person's samples. Don't put a person's name to experiences, opinions or anecdotes they didn't give you. Ask, or leave a `[NEED: the story behind this]` placeholder.

## 5. Launch posts (Product Hunt, LinkedIn, X, communities)

- Lead with what it does and for whom, in one plain sentence. Then why you built it, as one true specific story, if the user gave you one.
- Show it: a screenshot or demo description, and one concrete example of use.
- Be honest about stage and limits: "It's early. Export to Xero isn't there yet."
- Ask for one specific thing: try it, give feedback on X, or upvote where that's the norm.
- Skip "Thrilled to announce", "after months of hard work", and gratitude lists unless the voice really does that.

## 6. Final checks

- [ ] Character counts are within limits, shown next to each ad asset.
- [ ] Every headline makes sense alone (search ads).
- [ ] Variants differ in angle, not just wording.
- [ ] The hook carries a specific (number, name, moment, audience) and no hook template.
- [ ] No invented results, anecdotes or opinions attributed to a real person.
- [ ] No Markdown on platforms that don't render it; emoji only if the voice uses them, never as bullet points.
